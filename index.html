Precision Fermentation

Precision fermentation allows the production of milk and egg proteins and other ingredients using microorganisms, such as yeasts or fungi, instead of animals

Like cultivated and plant-based meat, protein derived from fermentation is better for the planet, people, and animals

Precision fermentation can reduce our reliance on industrial dairy farming, and its impact on the climate, the environment, and animals.

Invest in the future of food

HOW DOES IT WORK?

  • The genetic information is introduced into a culture of microorganisms (fungi or yeast)
  • Then they are grown in controlled fermentation tanks
  • Finally, the proteins are collected separating them from the microorganisms

The resulting proteins are identical to conventionally produced animal proteins and can be used to make a wide range of products (cheese, ice cream, milk, butter, protein powder, albumin etc)

IS IT A NEW TECHNOLOGY?

The use of genetically modified microbial strains is not new, it has been used for several decades in the field of medicine and food to produce :

  • the insulin for diabetics
  • rennet of cheese
  • vanillin
  • amylase in bread
  • the pectinase of fruit juices
  • vitamin supplements

In addition, there are numerous traditional fermented foods:
Tempeh, beer, yogurt, kimchi, sauerkraut and many others

"The second domestication"

First we domesticated macroorganisms (animals and plants).
Now we are starting to domesticate microorganisms.

Evolving from farming multicellular organisms to farming unicellular creatures
allows us to do far more with far less.

WHY IS IT IMPORTANT?


Sustainability

Industrial animal agriculture is the main cause of climate change: It emits between 14% and 51% of greenhouse gas emissions, more than the entire transportation sector

Industrial animal agriculture is also a leading cause of the most urgent environmental problems we face:

  • deforestation (80% global, 91% Amazon)
  • loss of biodiversity
  • use and pollution of water
  • desertification
  • eutrophication
  • dead ocean zones

Efficiency and saving of resources

It takes 9 calories to feed a chicken, to get 1 calorie of meat

That is, 9 times more land, 9 times more water, 9 times more resources per calorie than vegetable protein. With pork meat and cow meat, the waste of resources is even worse

77% of agricultural land is used to feed animals to produce meat, but this meat only provides 30% of the protein and 18% of the calories that people eat

Animal agriculture consumes 29% of agricultural water and contaminates underground water reserves with slurry

Demand for meat is expected to double by 2050. It is impossible to feed the growing world population with animal protein on just 1 planet

Health

70% of antibiotics are used on farm animals

  • This represents a huge risk, the possible "end of modern medicine":
    The creation of antibiotic-resistant superbugs
  • Experts predict that by 2050 antibiotic resistance will cause 10 million deaths per year

75% of contagious diseases are of zoonotic origin

The next pandemic will probably be caused by animal exploitation, just like Covid-19

Healthcare costs

Dairy and other precision fermented products do not contain:

  • cholesterol
  • antibiotics
  • hormones (estrogen, etc)
  • lactose, which makes them very attractive for people with allergies and intolerances
  • heavy metals, pus, micro plastics, etc
This makes them healthier than those of animal origin

Animal suffering

Every year, 75 billion farm animals (chickens, pigs, cows, etc) and 2.7 trillion marine animals are killed, to produce meat, dairy, fish and eggs

  • This has a huge impact on ecosystems, and causes a lot of unnecessary suffering
  • Awareness about the effects of animal exploitation is growing rapidly among the population, that is looking for alternatives that are more ethical, sustainable and healthier

Alternative proteins are the solution

These are the 4 main technologies that will define the future of food production:

Precision Fermentation

Based on microorganisms, it allows the production of proteins and other ingredients (fats, dyes, etc.) using yeasts and fungi

Biomass Fermentation

Based on fungal mycelium, it allows the production of mycoproteins of high nutritional value in a short time. Example: Quorn

Cultivated Meat

Cell-based, allows meat and fish to be produced bioidentical to the animal equivalent, using animal cells that reproduce in a bioreactor

Air Protein

Complete and natural protein, produced by non-GMO microorganisms, fed with water and air (hydrogen, oxygen, nytrogen, carbon)

"This will be the biggest disruption to the food system since the invention of agriculture"

Investment

In the first half of 2026, precision fermentation raised €100 million, exceeding the €97 million raised in all of 2025. Cultivated meat raised €18 million, including pet food. Sources: gfieurope.org, gfieurope.org

Public investment

European funders provided €1.3 billion for alternative-protein research in 2020–2025: 29% for fermentation and 9% for cultivated meat and seafood. The Dutch government awarded €60 million for a programme covering both technologies. Sources: gfieurope.org, cellulaireagricultuur.nl

More public investment is needed

Precision fermentation and cultivated meat have the potential to address challenges in food production, but more investment is needed to advance research, reduce costs and scale up production.

Governments can help by funding open-access research, supporting startups and building shared facilities that make it easier to bring new products to market. Sharing knowledge and encouraging collaboration can help more researchers and companies make progress.

Clear, science-based regulations are also essential to ensure food safety while giving innovators a predictable path to market.

Alternative proteins could help address several challenges at once, including climate change, environmental damage and animal welfare. Public and private investment can help turn that potential into practical, accessible food choices.

Ask your representatives to support public research and investment.

Help accelerate the future of food

Precision fermentation, biomass fermentation and cultivated meat need more investment to reach their full potential. Investors, policymakers and research funders can help move these technologies forward.
